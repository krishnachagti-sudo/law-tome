# Persuasion rules for the Law Tome books

What we use, what we use carefully, and what we never do. Source notes:
`research_notes/True or Folklore design/persuasion_catalogue.md` and
`research_notes/True or Folklore growth levers/consumer_psychology.md`.
The brand is "every claim checked", so a single exposed trick costs more than
it could ever earn. India's 2023 dark-pattern guidelines apply to Conyso as a
seller and advertiser, and regulators fined nine platforms in August 2026.

## Use

| Lever | Where it lives | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Guess before the answer | Tick boxes on every question page | Strong |
| Confident misses stick (hypercorrection) | SURE / GUESSING tick, "most confident miss" blank | Strong |
| Peak-end | Each round ends on its biggest surprise; book ends on the surprises page | Strong |
| Verdict and true fact first, myth second | Every answer page; every "Tell someone" line is the true fact | Strong (debunking research) |
| Loss-aversion framing, honestly | "Don't be the one at the table who repeats it" | Holds |
| Identity and status | Score titles from Easily Fooled to Myth Buster | Moderate |
| Repetition of brand marks | The verdict stamps on boxes, stamps, cards and cover | Holds (mere exposure) |
| Admitting limits (blemishing) | DISPUTED verdicts, errata page, "eighteen stories are still disputed" | Small but real |
| Reciprocity | Free round-1 sample for an email address only | Small effect |
| Real urgency | Amazon's published holiday ship-by dates; real Countdown Deals | Holds when true |

## Use with care

- **Social proof:** only real numbers, in the form "X% of N players fell for this", once the daily game has data. Until then, "widely believed", never "most people".
- **Streaks in the daily game:** offer a free freeze or repair; no guilt notifications.
- **Near-miss scores:** report them truthfully; never engineer them.
- **Review request:** one neutral ask after the surprises page; never route only happy readers to Amazon; nothing offered in exchange.

## Never

- Fake scarcity ("only 3 left": print on demand never runs out), fake countdowns, fake "was" prices.
- Fake, bought, swapped or family reviews; review gating.
- Pre-ticked add-ons, confirm-shaming decline links ("No thanks, I like being fooled"), forced phone or WhatsApp opt-ins, hidden subscriptions, nagging.
- "Bestselling", "free" or other authors' names in titles; reviews, prices or time-sensitive claims in the KDP description.
- Citing effects that failed replication: Zeigarnik, ego depletion, disfluent fonts, behavioural priming.
