# The Librarian: Law Tome series character bible

One character across every Law Tome book. Chosen by Krishna on 14 Nov 2026,
from option 2 of three mascot designs. Fixed reference images: `_series/mascot/model-*.png`.
Every image of her in every book uploads those sheets as references.

Name: **Ida Verity**, picked by Krishna on 16 Nov 2026. "Verity" is truth; with her cat Hearsay the pair is truth and rumour under one roof. The art files still say "librarian". She is introduced on the How to play page of True or Folklore?

---

## Who she is

She ran the reference desk of a big, old public library for forty years. Now
retired, she still can't hear a "fun fact" without asking where it came from.
For four decades she kept a card index of every claim a patron brought to her
desk and where it really came from. That index is the Law Tome.

She isn't a know-it-all. She's a *look-it-up*. Her authority comes from the
source on the card, never from being clever, and she'll tell you which drawer
it's in.

### Core traits

| Trait | How it shows |
|---|---|
| **Kind** | She never mocks the person, only the claim. Getting fooled is normal; staying fooled is optional. |
| **Dry** | Her humour is a raised eyebrow and a pause, not a punchline. |
| **Unimpressed by confidence** | The louder the claim, the slower she reaches for her magnifying glass. |
| **Delighted to be wrong** | Her favourite card is the one she had to correct. She keeps an "I was wrong" drawer and is proud of it. This is the series' honesty in one character, the same spirit as the errata page. |
| **Patient** | She will wait while you guess. She wants you to guess first. |
| **Stubborn about sources** | She never stamps a verdict without the card in her hand. No source, no stamp. |

### What she wants
For you to ask "says who?" by yourself, without her.

### What annoys her
"Everybody knows." Quotes on posters with no source. People who say "studies show" and can't name one.

### What delights her
A myth with a traceable first appearance. A disputed one that is *honestly* disputed. Readers who send corrections.

### Habits and mannerisms
- Peers over her half-moon glasses before she speaks.
- Taps her rubber stamp against her palm while thinking, like a judge's gavel she isn't allowed to use yet.
- Pulls index cards from her cardigan pocket; there are always more than should fit.
- Pushes her pencil deeper into her bun when a story gets good.
- Says "Hm." Means about six different things.

### Her sidekick: the cat
A round, fluffy, credulous cat who believes absolutely everything. It's the reader's
gullible side: it gasps at myths, sits on the wrong answer, falls asleep on
the sources. She loves it anyway. It gives her someone to raise an eyebrow at,
and gives each book a small running gag (the cat appears somewhere in every
round opener). Suggested name: **Hearsay**.

## How she looks (fixed: never change these)

**Silhouette test:** in solid black, she must still read as her: the bun with
a pencil through it, the glasses chain looping down both sides of her face,
the stamp in one hand.

- **Age and build:** about seventy. Small, a little round, upright posture, quick on her feet. About three heads tall in cartoon proportion.
- **Hair:** steel-grey (light grey fill), pulled into a tight round bun on top of her head, **one plain pencil pushed through the bun at an angle.** A couple of loose wisps at the temples.
- **Face:** round, soft, deep smile lines, a small sharp nose, expressive eyebrows (her main acting tool). Small round earrings.
- **Glasses:** half-moon reading glasses sitting low on her nose, on a **beaded chain** that loops down to her collar on both sides. She looks *over* them, not through them.
- **Clothes:** a light-grey cardigan, slightly too big, with **two front pockets stuffed with index cards** and buttons down the front; a white blouse with a round collar; a solid black knee-length skirt; solid black low-heeled sensible shoes.
- **Props (always the same):**
  - A **big rubber stamp** with a round wooden knob handle and a rectangular base. Its face is always blank in the art.
  - A **round magnifying glass** with a black handle.
  - Index cards (blank).
  - Sometimes a cloth tote bag of books.
- **Fills:** the house style: black outlines, solid black (skirt, shoes), one light grey (hair, cardigan). Pure white elsewhere.

### Never
- No top hat, monocle, bowler hat or anything that echoes an existing mascot.
- No colour (the covers' vermillion is for type, not for her).
- No text on her stamp, cards, bag or anything she holds.
- Never mean, sneering, or pointing and laughing at someone.
- Never shown being wrong *without* fixing it.

## Expression set (the six she always has)

1. **Over-the-glasses look:** the default. One eyebrow up, half-smile. "Says who?"
2. **The stamp slam:** decisive, eyes shut, tongue slightly out with effort. Verdict delivered.
3. **Magnifying squint:** one eye huge through the glass, the other screwed shut.
4. **Delighted:** hands clasped, eyes bright, when a story turns out stranger than the myth.
5. **"Hm.":** lips pressed, eyes sideways, thinking. For DISPUTED.
6. **Caught out:** sheepish grin, hand behind her head, pulling a card from the "I was wrong" drawer. For errata and corrections.
