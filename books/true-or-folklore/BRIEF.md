# True or Folklore? — book brief

**Status: draft for Krishna to edit.** The research says the brief is the one
part a human must own: it is what makes the book more than the site rearranged.
Change anything below that isn't what you'd say.

## Reader

A curious adult who has heard the famous stories: Murphy and the rocket sled,
the Cobra Effect, Newton's apple, the 38 witnesses who did nothing. They like
being the person at the table who knows the real version. They buy for
themselves or as a gift for someone like that. They are not academics and
will not read footnotes, but they want to know the answer can be checked.

## Promise

The stories behind famous laws, checked against the record. For each one you
guess first, then turn the page: true, folklore, or still disputed, and the
earliest source that settles it.

## What the book does that the site does not

1. **It makes you guess.** Every entry is a question before it is an answer.
   Guessing before the reveal improves recall (Kornell, Hays & Bjork 2009), and
   it turns the book into something people play together.
2. **It is curated and ordered.** About 100 stories chosen for fame and surprise,
   arranged in rounds that rise in difficulty, from 1,116 records.
3. **It explains why the myth spread.** The site corrects the record; the book
   also names the motive or mistake that made the false version travel.

## Rules the book keeps

- Three verdicts: TRUE, FOLKLORE, DISPUTED. Never force a binary.
- Each verdict rests on the earliest dated source in the record, named on the page.
- The fact comes first and last; the myth is clearly flagged as the popular
  version and never gets the last line (Debunking Handbook 2020).
- Anecdotes are labelled documented, reported or legend in the same sentence.
- A verdict about a living person is attributed to the evidence, never to the
  person's motives, and goes through the defamation pass.
- No "you" in the entry body (decision 2); the guessing prompt may address the reader.

## Shape

- About 100 entries in 8–10 rounds of 10–12, each round opening easy.
- 400–600 words per entry (proposal; set after the sample edit). Cap: 55,000 words.
- Each entry: question title → guess line (TRUE / FOLKLORE / DISPUTED?) → page turn →
  verdict stamp and the true fact in one line → the popular story → why it spread →
  the fact again, with the earliest source → a one-line source note.
- Party mode page at the front: everyone votes before the reveal, a point per right call.
- Back: score table, the sources page link, errata link, method note.

## Format

6×9 black-and-white paperback; reflowable Kindle edition with linked answers.
US spelling. Large verdict stamps in words, never colour alone. Where a famous
chart is the myth, the popular drawing marked "POPULAR VERSION" beside a
faithful redraw.

## Caps

55,000 words. Editing hours capped once the sample edit measures the rate.
