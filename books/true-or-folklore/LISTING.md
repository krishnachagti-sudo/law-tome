## Paperback description (Amazon, HTML allowed: `<b> <i> <br> <p> <h4>-<h6> <ul> <li>`)

Written for both people in a gift: the giver (the moment it's unwrapped) and the
recipient (what they'll still use in March).

```html
<h4>Did Newton get hit by an apple? Did Archimedes really shout "Eureka!" in the bath? Did Galileo drop balls from the Leaning Tower?</h4>
<p>Everyone has heard these stories. Fewer people know which ones actually happened.</p>
<p><b>True or Folklore?</b> puts 100 famous stories from science, history, money and the mind to the test. Each one starts as a question on its own page, with a cartoon and three boxes to tick: <b>TRUE</b>, <b>FOLKLORE</b> or <b>DISPUTED</b>. Then turn the page for the verdict, the real story, why the myth spread, and the earliest source that settles it.</p>
<h5>The gift</h5>
<ul>
<li>A page at the front to write who it's for</li>
<li>Score titles from "Easily Fooled" to "Myth Buster" to argue over</li>
<li>A "Tell someone" fact at the end of every answer, for the next dinner conversation</li>
</ul>
<h5>What they'll keep using</h5>
<ul>
<li><b>100 stories in 10 ready-made quiz rounds</b> for game nights, with a two-team scoresheet</li>

<li>A page of ideas for using it in class</li>
```
