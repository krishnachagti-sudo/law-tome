## How to play

{{how-diagram}}

Each story starts with a question on a right-hand page. Decide before turning over: **TRUE**, **FOLKLORE** or **DISPUTED**, and tick whether you are sure or guessing. The confident misses are the ones that stick.

- **TRUE**: the record supports the story as it is usually told.
- **FOLKLORE**: the record does not support it, or shows something different happened.
- **DISPUTED**: the evidence is real but still argued over, or too thin to settle it.

The answer starts overleaf, with the verdict in the first line and the earliest source that settles it near the end.

**Alone.** Keep score in the table at the back. A point for each right call.

**With others.** One person reads the question aloud. Everyone votes at once, by hand or on paper, before the page turns. A point for each right call; the reader reads the answer.

The book runs in ten rounds of ten. Each round opens with its best-known story and gets harder as it goes. Stars beside each question number are our estimate of how tricky it is: one star, a fair warm-up; two stars, it splits the room; three stars, a story almost everyone repeats. Don't be the one at the table who repeats it.

Every answer ends with a **Tell someone** line: the one fact worth repeating at dinner. Game-night rules, team play and classroom ideas are at the back.
