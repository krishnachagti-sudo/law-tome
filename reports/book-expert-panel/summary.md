1. **Frontispiece (p6) shows Franklin's kite and a key.** Not in the book.
2. **Verdict rules are applied unevenly.** "No early record" gives DISPUTED for Monte Carlo (№ 38) but FOLKLORE for Pareto (№ 37) and the Delhi cobras (№ 91); № 68 (Thucydides trap) says "the count is accurate" yet is DISPUTED; № 29 "Do we know what became of Little Albert?" doesn't map onto TRUE/FOLKLORE/DISPUTED. Fix: one printed line defining each verdict, then regrade against it (verdict changes need Krishna's approval).
3. **The game can be gamed.** 48 FOLKLORE / 34 TRUE / 18 DISPUTED; always saying FOLKLORE scores 48, and 9 of 10 in Round 2. Verdicts are facts and can't be rebalanced; the score bands can (e.g. Easily Fooled under 55).
4. **Cover at thumbnail size doesn't say "quiz".** Add a bold quiz band to the front.
5. **Classroom copying.** The copyright page lets readers copy only the score sheets; a line allowing single spreads for classroom use removes the main barrier for teachers (a rights decision).
6. **Tell someone lines** sometimes open with a hedge or the myth; experts want each fact-first, 25 words or fewer.
7. **Small print where readers write**: SURE/GUESSING boxes (~3 mm, 6 pt), the round-score line, 9.5 pt justified answers. Order a printed KDP proof before launch, which also checks the heavy black plates (pp. 13-14, 35-36, 239-240) for show-through.
8. **Sources**: Wikipedia is on ~94 source lines; a specialist reviewer will notice. List a primary source first where one is cited.
9. **One template, "the record" 210 times**: a light editing pass on repeated phrasing.
